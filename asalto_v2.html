  • Each unit deals fixed damage: it does not depend on the distance or on the castle.
  • The whole arsenal is placed, on empty cells. Never on a forest (the cells with a fir tree) and never on a castle.
  • No two units ever touch, not even diagonally.
  • The numbers along the top and the right are the units in each column and in each row.
  • No unit may sit idle: every one of them must damage at least one castle.
  • Each castle takes exactly its resistance. Overshooting is as invalid as falling short.
  • The archer shoots flat: between it and its target there can be no forest, no other unit and no cells of another castle.
  • The catapult shoots in an arc: it passes over forests and over other units. The only thing it cannot fly over is another castle.
  • With castles of several cells, range is measured to their nearest cell, and their cells cut the line of fire.
  • Every puzzle has at least one unit that reaches two castles at once: placing it well settles two counts in one go. In the dense puzzles, the ones with four castles or more, there are at least two.
  • No puzzle asks you to guess: all of them are finished by chaining deductions — counts, adjacency, arsenal and damage arithmetic, plus reasoning of the kind “if this one were a ram, that castle would overshoot”. Trying a placement and undoing it is never needed.
  • The fortress is always the castle on the board that takes the most. Between towers and watchtowers there is no fixed order: an unobstructed watchtower can hold out longer than a cornered tower.
